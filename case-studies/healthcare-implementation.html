Case study 02

Turning fragmented healthcare implementation into an operating system.

A generalized case study based on documented outcomes from pharmacy and payer implementation work. Employer, client, and patient information is intentionally excluded.

The context

Delivery had grown faster than the operating model around it.

Implementation work crossed customer success, product, data engineering, QA, operations, and leadership. Critical knowledge lived in people, spreadsheets, email, and inconsistent work items. That created hidden labor, uneven readiness, recurring defects, and weak cost visibility.

The intervention

I treated the delivery lifecycle as a product that needed redesign.

Mapped the current-state workflow and recurring failure points
Converted legacy ADO work into a structured Jira hierarchy and story-point model
Paired delivery effort with FTE cost analysis to create auditable COG visibility
Standardized intake, plans, SOPs, BRDs, PRDs, onboarding assets, and release gates
Partnered with engineering on Jenkins-based QA across development, staging, and production
Used SQL-backed root-cause analysis to convert recurring data issues into permanent fixes

Measured impact

The new system improved economics, quality, and scale.

25%Reduction in SaaS cost of goods
80%Reduction in manual UI QA effort
70+Pharmacy and payer implementations supported

The work also created the product-operational backbone needed to scale capacity without adding equivalent headcount.

Artifacts

What the operating system included.

  • Implementation intake and onboarding templates
  • Gantt plans and cross-functional milestone definitions
  • Jira hierarchy and cost governance model
  • Release-readiness criteria and escalation paths
  • Environment-aware automated QA
  • Data release SOPs and validation expectations
  • Confluence system of record with more than 160 pages of guidance
  • Post-launch issue patterns and permanent-fix feedback loops

Leadership signal

The value was not the documentation. It was the behavior the system enabled.

The artifacts made decisions, ownership, readiness, and cost visible. The stronger operating model gave customer-facing and technical teams a shared way to plan, execute, escalate, validate, and learn.
